Telecom Market & Supply Chain Bottlenecks

Semiconductor chips, battery cells, optical chips, and optical transceivers have become prominent industrial keywords. Driven by AI data center expansions, high-speed optical module demand is soaring. The global optical module market scale reached approximately 160 billion RMB in 2025 and continues to grow rapidly.

While China has become a manufacturing powerhouse for optical modules, it still faces critical bottlenecks in core components such as high-speed DFB and EML laser chips. US and Japanese giants (Coherent, Lumentum, Mitsubishi, Sumitomo) hold deep technological and patent barriers in 25G and above high-speed chips. While domestic suppliers have achieved breakouts at 2.5G/10G and some 25G products, high-end optical chips with higher bandwidth and reliability remain in a crucial catch-up phase.

Simulation & The Double-Loop Twin

Precision manufacturing requires design, and design requires simulation. High-end optoelectronic chip fabrication demands not only forward multi-physics numerical solvers but also closed-loop digital twin systems that unify forward design, physical testing, and inverse parameter calibration.

Once manufactured, every chip batch suffers from process fluctuations, material deviations, and packaging variances. Discrepancies between measured and simulated parameters contain both systematic batch drifts and random device variations. Therefore, modeling cannot stop at idealized forward simulations. It must leverage telemetry data to inversely calibrate internal parameters and feed them back to optimize the next design and tape-out cycle.

The Weather Forecasting Analogy

Early weather forecasting relied solely on historical observations. It later evolved to utilize numerical simulators based on physical laws. Modern meteorology, however, uses data assimilation—integrating physical numerical solvers with real-time sensor observations to continuously calibrate the system state. High-end chip design follows the same law: neither static empirical cases nor idealized numerical models are enough on their own. The optimal path is a digital twin combining physical models, manufacturing parameters, and test data for continuous self-calibration.

Overcoming Universal Tools

While universal tools like Crosslight PICS3D or Ansys Lumerical excel at forward analysis, they do not provide closed-loop digital twin frameworks mapped to specific production lines. High-speed chip autonomy requires resolving both "how to fabricate" and "how to characterize, calibrate, and continuously improve."

TLaser's Core Capabilities & Mission

TLaser was developed specifically to address these challenges. Its physical core, Lasers, is a high-performance opto-electro-thermal coupled simulation solver. Driven by the machine learning engine PLaser, it utilizes Physics-Informed Neural Networks, reduced-order models, and data distillation to convert high-fidelity multi-physics calculations into millisecond-scale digital twins.

1. Forward Prediction Computes laser behavior within a 7-dimensional parameter space in under 5 milliseconds to predict performance characteristics before packaging.
2. Inverse Calibration Ingests measured L-I-V telemetry from modules or chips to inversely resolve material, process, and equivalent design parameter drifts.
A Continuous Closed Loop: Design ➔ Simulation ➔ Manufacturing ➔ Testing ➔ Inverse Calibration ➔ Re-design. By bridging isolated pipelines, TLaser is seeking partners across DFB/EML chip fabs, optical module packagers, and testing research institutes to integrate real production telemetry. High-speed chip autonomy requires both localized supply chains and autonomous design simulation tools. TLaser commits itself to China's localized optical chip R&D.

Layer 1: Real Optical Transceiver Module

The optical module is the commercial, customer-facing product in the telecom hardware supply chain. It is purchasable, testable, and standardized.

  • Standardized Form Factors: Standard packaging (SFP/QSFP) plugged into switches and optical engines.
  • Measurable Performance Data: High-level parameters like total optical power, terminal voltage, and wavelength can be easily measured under bias sweeps.
  • The Challenge: Deep internal physical faults and parameter drifts cannot be directly inspected without breaking the module.

Layer 2: Internal Assembly & Subsystems

Decomposing the optical module reveals subassemblies coordinating electrical, optical, and thermal signals.

  • Optical Sub-Assembly (TOSA): Integrates the laser chip and couples its emission to fiber outputs.
  • Regulating Blocks: Miniaturized laser driver circuit, photodiode monitor path, and thermoelectric coolers (TEC).
  • Subsystem Interactions: Changes in driver impedance or thermal resistance directly affect the device's operational efficiency.

Layer 3: Laser Chip & Waveguide Geometry

Inside the TOSA package sits the actual semiconductor laser chip—the core light source.

  • Active Quantum Wells: A nanoscale waveguide layer where electrical carrier injection generates photons.
  • Resonant Cavity: Cleaved mirror facets (FP) or DBR gratings feedback light longitudinally.
  • Geometrical Parameters: Cavity length ($L$), ridge waveguide width ($w$), and active thickness ($d$) dictate electrical threshold and gain.

Layer 4: PLaser Forward Physical Simulator

PLaser provides the forward physical solver. It answers the forward question: given a design and parameters, what physical output profiles occur?

  • Carrier Dynamics: Resolves longitudinal spatial hole burning and carrier transport rate equations.
  • Photon Fields: Solves electromagnetic wave intensity propagation profiles along the active cavity.
  • Thermal Couplings: Integrates self-heating and mirror thermal loss, rendering rollover characteristics.

Layer 5: TLaser Digital Twin & Inverse Decisions

TLaser acts as the reverse calibration solver. It solves the inverse question: given measured SFP module LIV data, what are the internal parameter values?

  • LIV Ingestion & Schema Check: Validates real-time telemetry inputs under standards-compliant constraints.
  • Calibration Minimizer: Employs L-BFGS-B parameters solver to fit unmeasurable drifts (Rth, Rs, alpha_i).
  • Engineering Feedback Loop: Synchronizes digital twins instantly to predict failure boundaries and support design decisions.

7D Real-Time Neural Net Surrogate

TLaser replaces traditional slow numerical solvers with a 7D Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) surrogate model. By natively parameterizing the physical geometry, the twin instantly estimates distributions without mesh latencies.

The surrogate network maps a 7-dimensional input vector representable as: $$\mathbf{X} = [R_1, R_2, L, T_0, I_{\text{active}}, w_{\text{active}}, d_{\text{active}}]$$ to a 105-dimensional output vector, consisting of output power ($P_{\text{opt}}$),電光转换效率 ($WPE$), total terminal current ($I_{\text{total}}$), and 51-point longitudinal carrier density $N(z)$ and optical power $P(z)$ arrays.

Performance Acceleration: Prediction latency drops from seconds to < 5 milliseconds with an $R^2$ accuracy above 0.997 compared to the high-fidelity simulator.

Carrier & Photon Propagation Penalties

To enforce physical reality, TLaser integrates physical governing differential equations directly into the backpropagation pathway during surrogate training. This ensures conservation laws are satisfied even near threshold limits.

  • Carrier Continuity Residual: penalizes deviations from the steady-state rate equations: $$G_{\text{inj}} - R_{\text{rec}}(N(z)) - R_{\text{stim}}(N(z), P_{\text{tot}}(z)) = 0$$
  • Photon Propagation Residual: enforces the second-order wave intensity propagation along the cavity length: $$\frac{d^2P}{dz^2} - (\Gamma g(z) - \alpha_i)^2 P(z) = 0$$
Differentiable Loss: Integrating these constraints prevents non-physical oscillations, yielding smooth longitudinal distributions that strictly follow optical gain boundaries.

Online Parameter Optimization Loop

Due to environmental changes and manufacturing tolerances, unmeasurable physical parameters drift. TLaser establishes an active calibration process by comparing the predicted L-I-V curves against real-time measured inputs.

The optimizer uses an L-BFGS-B minimizer to fit the following variables: $$\boldsymbol{\theta} = [\alpha_i, \Gamma, C_{\text{multiplier}}, R_{\text{series}}, R_{\text{shunt}}]$$

  • Optical Loss $\alpha_i$ & Confinement $\Gamma$: shapes the threshold current and differential slope efficiency.
  • Auger Multiplier $C$: dictates the non-radiative recombination temperature droop.
  • Series $R_s$ & Shunt $R_{\text{sh}}$: aligns the terminal voltage curves including current leakage paths.

VCSEL Extension Demonstration

Simplified physical validation and demonstration of Vertical-Cavity Surface-Emitting Laser (VCSEL) digital twin workflows.

TLaser introduces a dedicated VCSEL Extension Mode to demonstrate numerical simulation and online calibration for vertical-cavity lasers. This mode is physically separate from the edge-emitting (DFB/FP) models and utilizes a custom solver based on a cylindrical radial coordinate system.

Key VCSEL-specific physics modeled in this extension include:

  • Aperture Diameter: Adjusting the oxide layer opening to control optical confinement and current density distributions.
  • Radial Current Crowding: Simulating how injection current peaks near the oxide aperture boundary.
  • Radial Spatial Hole Burning: Modeling carrier depletion at the aperture center due to intense stimulated transitions.
  • Self-Heating & Thermal Rollover: Calibrating the optical power drop caused by junction heating and top/bottom Distributed Bragg Reflector (DBR) losses.
Reduced Physics Notice: The current VCSEL modeling mode operates under a reduced-physics rate equation model. It serves as a digital twin pipeline demonstration until validated against wafer-level LIV and spectral measurements.

Methodology & Physics

Physics-Informed Neural Network with dynamic geometry inputs.

TLaser integrates the high-fidelity semiconductor physics from the Lasers core into a real-time digital twin. During dataset sweeps, the local active area cross-section $A_{\text{act}} = w_{\text{active}} \times d_{\text{active}}$ is varied, which scales both carrier generation rates and stimulated optical transitions.

This allows the digital twin to accurately predict Spatial Hole Burning profiles and electro-optical efficiency metrics for any ridge size without needing custom grids or rebuilding simulator boundaries.
